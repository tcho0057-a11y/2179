An analytical visualization of Australian tourism dynamics, contrasting domestic and international travel patterns across different states, seasons, and regional preferences. This dashboard tracks how state populations alter outward travel behavior, visualizes the seasonal rhythms of holiday migration trends, and breaks down total economic impact to uncover whether residents prefer exploring localized regional gems or departing for major international hubs.

1 Which State Travels the Most?
By State of Residence

NSW and VIC dominate raw outbound travel volumes but population size drives those numbers up.
Adjust for residents, and the story shifts. WA jumps to second punching well above its weight.

ACT and NT also outperform their small populations.
Tasmania sits last on both raw and per-capita measures.
Where you live strongly predicts how much you travel.

2 Where Do Australians Go Overseas?
International Destinations

New Zealand leads close, familiar, and easy to reach. Indonesia follows, driven by Bali's enduring mass appeal.
Japan surged post-pandemic, now firmly in the top three.

Vietnam, China, and India reflect growing diaspora travel demand.
The USA is the only non-Asia-Pacific entry in the top ten. Eight of ten destinations sit within the Asia-Pacific region.

Distance, cost, and culture all pull Australians northward and east.
Short flights and visa-free access make the region hard to resist.

3 When Do Australians Travel?
Seasonality

Australian overseas travel peaks sharply around summer and Christmas. January 2026 recorded the highest departures in the dataset.
December follows closely the holiday exodus is clearly visible.

Travel dips through September and October before climbing again. The November surge marks the start of the summer rush.
February and March remain elevated as late-summer travel continues. School holidays and long weekends visibly shape departure patterns.
Seasonal rhythm, not chance, drives when Australians choose to leave.

Holiday periods create visible spikes in overseas departures
4 Overseas vs. Staying Home
DOMESTIC VS INTERNATIONAL (TWO SOURCES)

Australians are active travellers, moving freely both within their borders and abroad. Domestic overnight travel remains dominant, yet international departures are steadily climbing.
Global travel continues to normalise, and Australians are leaving in record numbers.
Larger states naturally lead total activity NSW, Victoria, and Queensland drive outbound volumes.

Population size is the strongest predictor of how much a state travels overall.
Smaller regions tell a different story, displaying unique behaviours shaped by local culture.
Across the board, Australians balance a love of local exploration with a growing appetite for overseas travel.

NSW dominates both domestic and international travel activity
Domestic overnight travel remains significantly larger overall
5 Who Takes Longer Domestic Trips?
Trip Length and Age

Older Australians dominate longer domestic trips as the pattern sharpens with every extra night. Travellers aged 60+ make up half
of all trips lasting 15 nights or more. Younger travellers aged 14–29 prefer short stays, peaking at 1–3 night trips.
The 30–44 group stays relatively consistent across all trip lengths.
Age is the single strongest predictor of how long Australians travel domestically.

Travellers aged 60+ make up half of trips lasting 15 nights or longer
6 Where Do Domestic Travellers Spend Most?
Domestic Expenditure

Domestic tourism yield follows a highly centralized pattern. Australia's eastern seaboard which is anchored by corporate travel hubs
in NSW and Victoria alongside Queensland's leisure market accounts for the overwhelming majority of national visitor expenditure.

NSW records the highest domestic tourism expenditure
7 Why Do Australians Travel?

Holiday travel accounts for over a third of all domestic trips. Visiting friends and relatives is the second largest purpose at 21.5%.
Together, they make up well over half of all travel.

Business travel is surprisingly small - just 5% of total trips. Australians travel domestically for leisure far more than work.
The December quarter amplifies holiday and social travel significantly. Other purposes - education, events, health - account for 7.6%.

Personal connection and recreation are what move Australians around.
